# Discovery — For the User Who Genuinely Cannot Name a Domain

> ## ⚠️ Read this before anything else in this file
>
> **This file is the rare branch, not the front door.** It runs **only** when the user has already been asked which domain the workbench manages **and could not answer** — 「我也不知道」·「你帮我定」·「就是想弄个什么，没想好」.
>
> **If he hasn't been asked yet, you are in the wrong file.** Go back to SKILL.md §0.D and ask the one routing question. Most people who open with 「我想做个个人工作台」 know perfectly well what they want to manage; they just haven't said it. Running a life inventory on them costs a round, reads as an interrogation, and produces nothing that one word wouldn't have.
>
> **If he names a domain, you leave immediately** — Door Q or N, and the V0 ships that turn. **If the domain is a system one** (客户 · 订单 · 库存 · Agent · 设备 · 一堆数据要盯着), you leave for `system-domain.md`, not for a starter, and its two questions replace the evidence questions below.
>
> **This file is personal-domain only, and that is not an oversight.** The system domain has no equivalent blank case: someone who says 「我们想做个后台」 without knowing what it manages doesn't need a life inventory, he needs 「围着什么转」 — `system-domain.md` §0's first question. Reverse-inference works here because a personal workbench lives inside one person's week. A company's workaround (三个 Excel 在群里传, 五个后台来回切) is still worth hearing, but it rides in the *optional* slot of the routing message (SKILL.md §0.D1); it never becomes a six-question archaeology.

Everything below is for the genuinely blank case. It is worth doing well, because that user really can't be routed and the evidence method really does find something a menu never would. **It is also perhaps one session in five.**

> **The method in one line: stop asking what he needs, and go find what he's already doing badly.** — that line is for you. It is not an opening statement, a framing device, or something he benefits from hearing. §0 is what he actually receives.

---

## 0. The opening message — copy this shape

**Everything after §0 in this file is reasoning material for you.** It explains why the questions are these questions and how to read the answers. **None of it goes to the user**, and the most common failure of this whole skill is a model that reads this file top to bottom and hands back a well-organized summary of it (SKILL.md §0.H).

What he gets is short and starts working immediately:

```
那我换个问法 —— 问你两件过去的事，比问你想要什么准：

1. {question, from §2}
2. {question, from §2}

答一条就够，我拿它给你一个具体的台子：有名字、有你每天打开会看到的
那句话、有它每天占你几秒。你对着实物改，比现在空想准。
```

**Four rules that make this work:**

1. **The first sentence is the first question**, or the one clause that sets it up. No preamble. Not a diagnosis of his request, not what you're about to do, not why this approach beats another one.
2. **No reasons attached.** Every question in §2 is self-evidently purposeful read cold; explaining what it hunts (「凑合用的工具说明需求已经被验证过」) turns a question he'd answer into a lecture he'll skim.
3. **Say what he gets back, in one sentence, in concrete nouns** — 名字 · 每天看到的那句话 · 每天几秒. That closing line is the only self-description permitted, because it tells him what an answer buys.
4. **Two questions, and「答一条就够」.** He already failed to answer one question; do not respond by asking three more.

Target length: **the block above, ±30%.** A draft at triple it is method narration — cut that, not the questions.

**The moment one answer arrives, you owe him the whole V0** (SKILL.md §0.C), that turn. Do not follow up with question three. Even a partial answer is enough to assert from, and asserting wrong gets corrected faster than asking right gets answered.

## 1. The category menu is banned as an opening move

The reflex is to offer a list of domains. It feels generous. It is the most reliable way to waste the session, for three compounding reasons:

1. **He picks the first plausible row.** Not the truest one — the first one that isn't embarrassing. You now have a category, not a person.
2. **You learn nothing.** A category tells you the domain and nothing about his week: not his moment, not his tolerance for logging, not the tool he's already using. Every later decision — hook, input budget, channel mix — has to be guessed.
3. **You build the generic one.** Given only a category name, you will produce that category's median workbench, which is what he could have downloaded. The thing he asked for was *his*.

This is the same disease `finesse-ui` treats with divergence, one level up: a closed list of N options yields N products, and the population converges on two of them.

### 1.A The words themselves are the hazard — including in a refusal

> **Never write a domain name he did not write first.** Not as an example, not as a counter-example, not after 「比如」, and not inside a sentence declining to offer them. The ban is on **the words reaching him**, not on their being formatted as a list.

The polite version of the violation is the common one: a sentence that announces it isn't giving a menu, and names three domains in the parenthetical explaining why. Those three are now in his head, and the next message is often him picking one — failure #1 above, reached by a more articulate route.

**And don't explain the ban either.** 「菜单只会让你随手点第一行，然后做出一个谁都能下载的通用款」 is §1 of this file recited to the person it protects (SKILL.md §0.H). He never needed to know a menu was an option. Open with §0. Those questions read as purposeful with no preamble justifying them.

> **The one legal use of a list** is *late* and *narrow*: after the evidence points at a domain, naming two or three concrete workbenches **within that domain** to sharpen a fork ("是想管训练，还是管身体变化？"). That's a fork, not a menu — it comes from his evidence and each branch is something you'd genuinely build differently.

## 2. The six evidence questions

Ask **two or three**, never all six — six is an interview, and an interview makes him perform an answer. Pick by what he's already said. Every one of them asks about *behavior in the past*, never about *desire in the future*. Drop them into §0's block **verbatim** — they are already worded for the user; rewriting them is where the explanations creep back in.

| # | Ask | What it's really hunting | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 有没有什么事，你现在是拿备忘录、Excel、微信收藏夹或者跟自己发消息在凑合着做的？ | **The makeshift tool** | ★★★★★ |
| 2 | 有没有你试过、但用了不到两周就删掉的 App 或者表格？为什么删的？ | **The abandoned attempt** — names the exact input cost that broke him | ★★★★★ |
| 3 | 哪个 App 你几乎天天开，但每次开都有点烦？ | **The resented app** — category validated, shape wrong | ★★★★ |
| 4 | 有没有什么事你每隔一阵就要重新搜一遍，永远记不住？ | **The re-googled question** | ★★★ |
| 5 | 翻一下你手机相册，最近最多的是什么照片？ | **The photographed thing** — an unlabeled log already being kept | ★★★ |
| 6 | 上周你为什么事查过资料、算过账，或者担心过？ | **The recurring worry** | ★★★ |

### Why #1 and #2 outrank everything else

**A makeshift tool is a fossil of a validated need.** Someone maintaining a 备忘录 he keeps re-editing has already proven three things no survey can: the need is real, it recurs, and he will pay a daily tax for it. Your job shrinks from "invent a product" to "replace a known-bad tool", which is a far better position.

**An abandoned attempt is even more precise, because it tells you the failure boundary.** "那个健身 App 每组都要点四下，烦" is a hard number: his daily input budget is *below* that. No amount of asking "你能接受每天录多少" produces that, because nobody knows their own tolerance until they've broken it. Whenever you get an abandonment story, **the input budget of the new workbench is set below the one that broke him** — and say so out loud in the Workbench Read.

### Reading the answers

- **"没有" to all of them** is information, not a dead end — it usually means no domain currently costs him anything daily, so a *daily* workbench is the wrong cadence. Drop to CADENCE 3–5 and look for a weekly or event-driven surface (报税、体检、旅行、季度复盘). A weekly workbench he opens is worth more than a daily one he doesn't.
- **An answer about someone else** (孩子、宠物、爸妈) is a **care** structure, and it's a strong one — logging another being's state is easier than logging your own, and the emotional return is higher.
- **A work answer** ("我每天要统计店里流水") is **operation**. Check first whether he wants a personal tool or a business tool; the latter has other stakeholders and leaves this skill's scope (SKILL.md §9).

## 3. Form a hypothesis early, then assert it

You do not need six data points. **Two pieces of evidence plus a structure guess is enough to assert.** Asserting a specific wrong workbench gets you a fast, informative correction ("不是训练，我其实想管体重"); asking a fourth question gets you a fourth polite answer.

The inference chain, in order:

```
evidence → domain → structure (7 types) → the moment → the hook → the input budget → the rail
```

Do not skip **the moment**. 早上通勤 / 睡前 / 练完那一下 / 收摊后 — it constrains the hook's tense and the input's size, and a workbench with no moment has no cadence (SKILL.md §4).

## 4. The Workbench Read

Format, rules and a worked example: **SKILL.md §0.C**. Three things people get wrong when writing it:

1. **The hook must carry real values, not a template.** `今天是{周期}第{n}天` is unfalsifiable and he will approve it. `今天是黄体期第 3 天` forces you to notice that something has to produce "3".
2. **Quote his own words back in 我注意到.** It's the difference between a proposal and a horoscope, and it's what makes him trust the rest of the block.
3. **State the daily cost in seconds and physical actions.** Especially when he has an abandonment story — that's the number he'll judge against, and he's the only one who can.

Then **stop**. Nothing further gets defined until he answers.

## 5. When the evidence points at more than one workbench

Common, and not a problem — **as long as you don't merge them.** The merge is the failure: 「健康工作台」 covering 经期 + 睡眠 + 情绪 + 运动 has no single hook, no single moment, and four separate input taxes. It becomes an app, then it becomes nothing.

Name the fork plainly and make him choose one to build **first**:

```
你的情况其实指向两个不同的台子，它们没法合成一个（每天开的时间点都不一样）：

① 睡眠台 —— 睡前开，记一次入睡时间，早上告诉你昨晚怎么样
② 情绪台 —— 白天随手记，周末给你一张这周的起伏图

先做哪个？另一个我给你留在 spec 里，做完这个再接。
```

**Say why they can't merge** (different moments, different input rhythms) — otherwise "先做一个" sounds like laziness rather than a design decision. And genuinely record the second one in the spec's `deferred` field so the promise is real.

---

## 6. When he asks you to just pick

"你帮我定就行" is a real answer, not an evasion. Take it — **pick the one with the strongest evidence, say which evidence made you pick it, and keep the veto open**:

> 那我选养宠这个，因为你相册里全是猫，说明你已经在记录了，只是没地方放。
> 如果这不是你最想要的，说一声，第二顺位是记账。

Picking with a stated reason is assertive and correctable. Picking silently is a guess he can't audit.

---

## 7. What to hand forward

When the Workbench Read is confirmed, discovery is done. Carry these into `grammar.md` — all six, written down:

- the **evidence** (it goes in the spec, and it's what makes the workbench feel like his);
- the **domain** and the **structure** (primary + secondary, `workbench-types.md`);
- the **moment**;
- the **input budget** — in seconds, and below any abandonment threshold he reported;
- the **hook sentence**, with real values;
- anything he **vetoed** — it belongs in the spec's `excluded` field so it doesn't creep back in later.
